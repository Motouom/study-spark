# Coordinate Geometry

Coordinate geometry combines algebra and geometry to describe lines, circles, and curves using equations. The GCE paper tests distance, midpoint, gradients, and intersections.

### What you must know

- **Gradient (slope) of a line:** $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$. Parallel lines have equal gradients; perpendicular lines have gradients that multiply to $-1$.
- **Equation of a line:** Gradient-intercept form $y = mx + c$, where $m$ is the gradient and $c$ is the $y$-intercept. Point-gradient form: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
- **Distance between two points:** $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$.
- **Midpoint of a line segment:** $M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$.
- **Equation of a circle:** $(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2$, where $(a, b)$ is the centre and $r$ is the radius.
- **Circle properties:** A line from the centre to a chord bisects the chord; the angle in a semicircle is a right angle.

### Worked example

**Find the equation of the line passing through $A(1, 2)$ and $B(3, 6)$.**

Step 1: Find the gradient: $m = \frac{6 - 2}{3 - 1} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$.

Step 2: Use point-gradient form with $A(1, 2)$: $y - 2 = 2(x - 1)$.

Step 3: Simplify to gradient-intercept form: $y = 2x$.

### Worked example 2

**Find the centre and radius of the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$.**

Step 1: Complete the square for $x$: $x^2 - 6x = (x - 3)^2 - 9$.

Step 2: Complete the square for $y$: $y^2 + 4y = (y + 2)^2 - 4$.

Step 3: Rewrite the equation: $(x - 3)^2 - 9 + (y + 2)^2 - 4 - 12 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25$.

Step 4: Centre $(3, -2)$, radius $r = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

### Common mistakes to avoid

- Confusing the centre $(a, b)$ with $(-a, -b)$ in the circle equation.
- Forgetting to complete the square when finding circle properties.
- Mixing up the formulas for distance and midpoint.

### Practice questions

1. Find the gradient of the line through $(2, 3)$ and $(5, 11)$.
2. Find the midpoint of the segment joining $(-1, 4)$ and $(3, -2)$.
3. Find the distance between $(1, 2)$ and $(4, 6)$.
4. Write the equation of the circle with centre $(2, -3)$ and radius $4$.

### Answers

1. $m = \frac{11 - 3}{5 - 2} = \frac{8}{3}$.
2. $M = (1, 1)$.
3. $d = \sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (6 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$.
4. $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 16$.
